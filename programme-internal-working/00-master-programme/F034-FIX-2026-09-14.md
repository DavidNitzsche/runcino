Fixes the mechanism `WHY-PROPOSEADAPTIVEBUMP-NEVER-FIRES-2026-09-14.md`
diagnosed: `composePlan` (via `cycleBoundedPeak` in
`web-v2/lib/plan/generate.ts`) sized every freshly-authored peak week at the

`adaptive-ramp.ts`'s `belowTierUpper` gate recomputed its ceiling from the

authoring. Verified on all three of David's tier-banded plan versions:
authored peak sat at 99.7–99.8% of the recomputed ceiling every time. Per the
coach consultant's answer (`for coaching consult/consult-log/2026-09-14-008-
f034-adaptive-bump-fix-direction.md`), Option 1 is the only option
authorized: **reserve headroom at authoring time**, not weaken the gate
(Option 2), not loosen the quality-session evidence bar (Option 3), and not
dampen rebuild cadence (Option 4).

## 1 · The exact fix

### 1.1 `web-v2/lib/plan/load-progression-contract.ts`

Added a new exported constant and an optional parameter to the ONE shared
peak-bound function, `plannedPeakBound`:

```ts
export const AUTHORING_HEADROOM_RESERVE_SHARE = 0.06;
```

```ts
export function plannedPeakBound(args: {
  readonly demonstratedPeakWeeklyMi: Measured;
  readonly climbFromMi: Measured;
  readonly climbWeeksToPeak: number | null;
  readonly distanceFloorMi: number;
  readonly reserveHeadroomShare?: number;   // NEW · defaults to 0
}): LoadReading {
  ...
  const reserveShare = args.reserveHeadroomShare ?? 0;
  const cycleBoundMi = peak * PER_CYCLE_PEAK_GROWTH * (1 - reserveShare);
  // ...unchanged floor/reachable clamps, applied AFTER the reserve...
  const basis: LoadBasis =
    bounded === floorMi && floorMi > Math.min(cycleBoundMi, reachableMi)
      ? (peak >= args.distanceFloorMi ? 'demonstrated_peak_floor' : 'distance_floor')
      : reachableMi < cycleBoundMi
        ? 'reachable_by_weekly_growth'
        : reserveShare > 0
          ? 'per_cycle_growth_on_demonstrated_peak_reserved'   // NEW basis label
          : 'per_cycle_growth_on_demonstrated_peak';
  return known(bounded, basis, CITE_VOLUME_RULES);
}
```

**Defaults to 0 for every existing caller.** `resolveLoadProgressionContract`'s
`plannedPeakLoad` (the published `tier_peak_weekly_band` and the stamped
fallback ceiling) and `recomputeAdaptationCeiling` (the gate's live-evidence
recompute) both call `plannedPeakBound` WITHOUT setting this parameter, so
they are byte-for-byte unaffected by this change — they still compute the
full, unreserved bound. This is the entire mechanism of the fix: the CEILING
(what the gate measures against) stays full-strength; only the AUTHORED
number (what actually gets composed into the calendar) is reserved.

### 1.2 `web-v2/lib/plan/generate.ts`

The ONE authoring call site, `cycleBoundedPeak` (which sizes the actual
physical peak week `volumeCurve`'s geometric ramp climbs to), now passes the
reserve:

Confirmed this is the actual, sole authoring site (not a duplicate or a
different consumer) by tracing `cycleBoundedPeak`'s only call site
(`volumeCurve`'s `peakTarget`, `generate.ts:~3805`), which is what every
non-taper, non-overridden week's target mileage in the composed calendar
climbs toward. `peakTargetOverrideMi` (the maintenance/hold-block path,
HOLD-PROGRESS-1) bypasses `cycleBoundedPeak` entirely and is therefore
untouched by this change, as intended.

## 2 · Why 0.06, not 0.05 or 0.08

The consult log names `ADAPTATION_HEADROOM_SHARE` (0.05) as "a reasonable
starting point" but explicitly leaves the exact size to calibration
("implementation-detail number… not a coaching-doctrine question").

- **Not 0.05**: `belowTierUpper` is `peakHeadroomMi > ceiling ×
  ADAPTATION_HEADROOM_SHARE` — a STRICT `>`. Reserving exactly 0.05 lands
  day-one headroom EXACTLY on that boundary, a Rule 9 cliff sensitive to the
  0.1 mi rounding `volumeCurve` applies. Falsified: set the constant to 0.05
  and the new doctrine claim, and both new/updated `_load_progression_
  contract.test.ts` assertions, correctly failed (see §5).
- **Not 0.08** (my first candidate): calibrated empirically against
  `_sweep_allusers.test.ts`'s full 11,687-archetype corpus, not chosen from
  the boundary math alone. At 0.07–0.08, the `density:steady:marathon` probe
  (a FIXED 4 mi threshold session, priced independently of weekly volume)
  crosses `lib/plan/dosing.ts`'s weekly percentage-share cap — a FATAL,
  Rule-7-cited Daniels dosing-cap violation (`VALIDATOR[cold/strava]` FIRM
  failures). Measured precisely: **FIRM failures appear between reserve 0.065
  and 0.066** on that one archetype. This is a genuine, narrow, pre-existing
  dosing margin in that archetype (its fixed-mileage session doesn't re-scale
  with a shrinking week), not a new defect in the reserve mechanism itself,
  but it is real and it caps how large the reserve may safely be.
- **0.06** clears `ADAPTATION_HEADROOM_SHARE` by a full percentage point
  (0.01) with real absolute margin (0.2–0.9 mi at realistic 20–90 mi/wk
  ceilings — comfortably outside rounding noise) and sits with real distance
  below the 0.065–0.066 dosing-cap cliff. It also lands almost exactly on the
  consult log's own illustrative example: a block authored at `1.15 × 0.94 =
  1.081` — **~8.1% growth over demonstrated peak instead of the full 15%** —
  matching the consult log's "~9% growth… instead of the full 15%" framing.

## 6 · Real regressions found and how each was handled

### 6.1 `easyfloor:steady:half` — FIXED

`_sweep_allusers.test.ts`'s Rule-15 coverage check (`easy:floor-binds` must
be reached by at least one archetype) broke: reserving 6% of the peak week
shrinks the "remainder" budget `layoutWeek` splits across easy days, and at
`weeklyFrequency: 6` that remainder split six ways dropped below
`easyfloor:steady:half`'s demonstrated-easy-day floor for BOTH arcs of the
probe pair (making `easyMileFloor` look inert for the one family
`EASYFLOOR_RULE12_EXEMPT` names as non-exempt). At `weeklyFrequency: 5` the
same remainder splits five ways, each easy day is bigger, and the floor
demonstrably binds again (`raised.peakEasyMi` 9 > `measured.peakEasyMi` 8) —
a genuine, real-shaped runner, not a number picked to satisfy the gate.
Documented in `sim-matrix.ts` at the change site. Full corpus (11,687
archetypes) re-verified green, including the dosing sweep gate.

### 6.2 `_rolling_seven_ceiling.test.ts` — NOT FIXED, flagged for follow-up

Two assertions in this file fail on top of this branch:

FAIL it takes EASY miles only · no long run, quality session or race day is cut
  the fixture never breached the ceiling — it cannot show what is protected: expected 0 to be greater than 0

FAIL Rule 9 · a hair more demonstrated peak buys a hair more plan, not a different one
  the peak rolling-7 jumped on a hair of demonstrated peak: expected 2.5 to be less than or equal to 1

This file's fixture (`cimInput()`, `peakMi: 52.3`, a 15-week block starting
`2026-08-24` — the exact reference runner tied to the real S1.5 load-audit
incident that motivated `ROLLING7-1`) is calibrated to sit JUST over its own

With the authoring reserve applied, the composed block shrinks enough
(peak week 60 → ~57) that this SAME fixture's rolling-7 exposure no longer
exceeds the ceiling at all — the trim mechanism (`enforceRollingSevenCeiling`)
never engages, so the liveness assertion ("a trim was needed, to prove what's
protected") has nothing to prove.

More concerning: walking `peakMi` from 52.0 to 52.6 in 0.1 mi steps shows a
genuine 2.5 mi jump between 52.2 (rolling-7 = 57.0) and 52.3 (rolling-7 =
59.5) in the UNTRIMMED composed output. I instrumented `enforceRollingSevenCeiling`
directly (temporary diagnostic, not committed) and confirmed:

- This jump is **insensitive** to `recentLongMi`, `spikeAnchorLongMi`,
  `demonstratedLongMi`, `recentWeeklyMi`, `rampBaseEvidence.heldMi/baseMi/
  sustainedMi`, and `easyDayMedianMi` — none of these inputs move it.
- `load-progression-contract.ts`'s own continuity tests (`_load_progression_
  contract.test.ts` G3/G3b, which walk `plannedPeakBound` directly in 0.01 mi
  steps) STILL PASS cleanly with this fix — so the shared arithmetic this
  branch touches is smooth. The discontinuity lives downstream, in
  `volumeCurve`/`layoutWeek`'s day-by-day placement (most likely a
  deload/cutback week or long-run-day-of-week boundary flipping at a
  specific peakMi threshold for this specific timeline).
- This is very plausibly a **pre-existing** discontinuity, previously masked:
  before this fix, `enforceRollingSevenCeiling` ALWAYS engaged for this
  fixture across the whole walked range (it always trimmed down to exactly
  the ceiling), so the OUTPUT was smooth by construction even if the
  underlying untrimmed shape was not. This fix legitimately opens enough
  headroom that the trim mechanism now only sometimes engages, exposing
  whatever shape irregularity was already there.

I spent substantial effort trying to recalibrate this ONE fixture (long-run
inputs, sustained/held mileage, race-timeline length from 6 to 15 weeks) to
find a combination that both breaches its ceiling and stays smooth across the
walked range, without success within a reasonable effort budget — the
excess appears to depend on internal `layoutWeek` rounding/cadence

this fixture exposes.

**I did not touch `AUTHORING_HEADROOM_RESERVE_SHARE`, `plannedPeakBound`, or
