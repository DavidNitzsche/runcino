# Defending the proposed block against David's own training history

The challenge was right: a schedule looking aggressive does not make it wrong.
This document tests each materially challenged session against **his completed
runs**, not against a research table. Every number is read from production.

Three verdict classes are used throughout and never blurred:

- **ALLOWED** — a research table permits it. Says nothing about him.
- **SUPPORTED** — he has completed a comparable session, and it went well.
- **CONDITIONAL** — it depends on evidence that does not exist yet.

## The four challenged sessions

### 1 · 2026-11-01 — 21.5 mi with a 5-mile fast finish at M

**The structure is SUPPORTED. The distance is not.**

He has executed a fast-finish long run **twice**, and both went well:

**06-27 · 14.02 mi**, splits 8:44 8:16 8:15 8:12 8:27 8:29 *10:07* 7:50 8:05 8:10
**7:03 7:20 7:28 7:06** 8:06 — miles 11–14 average **7:14/mi**, which is
**38 s/mi FASTER than his 7:52 marathon target**. HR through that block:
147 → 166 → 169 → 154. It rose into the work and came back down. No collapse.

**07-25 · 18.00 mi**, splits show a genuine progression to 7:28 7:30 7:40 7:56
7:46 across miles 10–14 — **five miles at or under MP, inside an 18-mile run.**
HR 134 at the start, 164–166 through the fast section, 161–162 at the finish.
Faster *and* higher HR in the second half: that is a deliberate progression, not
decoupling.

**So a 5-mile MP finish is not novel for him. He has already done it, at 18
miles.** The preview's addition is well-founded.

**What is NOT supported is 21.5 itself.** It is **+19% on his 2026 maximum**, and
the one time he reached that maximum:

```
07-25   18.00 mi
07-27 week   PLAN ASKED 52 mi   ·   HE RAN 4.2 mi
             (1.34 + 0.84 + 1.98 on 08-01, all inside 30 minutes — one broken
              recording, not three sessions — then six days of nothing)
```

I cannot prove the 18-miler caused that week; travel, work or illness would look
identical and none of it is in the data. But the fact stands on its own: **the
only time he has gone this deep, the following week did not happen.**

**Verdict: ALLOWED and structurally SUPPORTED, but the distance is CONDITIONAL.**
21.5 with a fast finish stacks a never-run distance on top of a proven structure.
**Recommendation: keep the fast finish, size it to 3 miles rather than 5, and
make the 5-mile version conditional on the 10-18 twenty-miler being completed at
≥95% with no late fade.** That earns the novelty rather than assuming it.

### 2 · The 2026-10-26 peak week — three stressors, 60.0 mi

This is **already the live plan**, not a proposed change:

```
Mon  easy 4.5 (8×20s strides)      Fri  easy 8.0
Tue  tempo 10.0 · 6 mi @ T         Sat  rest
Wed  easy 7.5 (8×20s strides)      Sun  LONG 21.5
Thu  intervals 8.5 · 9×3 min @ I   ─────────────────
                                    TOTAL 60.0 mi
```

**NOT SUPPORTED by his data.**

| | his demonstrated max | this week asks | delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| weekly volume | **47.5 mi** | 60.0 | **+26%** |
| longest run | **18.0 mi** | 21.5 | **+19%** |
| stressors in one week | 2 | **3** | — |

He has never run a 60-mile week. He has never run 21.5. The week asks him to do
both at once, plus 6 mi at T and 9×3 min at I. Every component is ALLOWED by
`Research/22`'s marathon-intermediate row; **none of the three peaks is
SUPPORTED by anything he has completed.**

Judged as a sequence rather than session by session, this is the weakest week in
the block: it is the single point where volume, longest run and stressor count
all peak together.

**Recommendation — the minimum necessary revision:** drop ONE stressor. Keep the
21.5 and the 6 mi at T; move the 9×3 min at I to the following week or replace
it with an easy day. That takes the week to ~54 mi (+14% on his max rather than
+26%) and leaves two stressors, which is the pattern he has actually absorbed.

### 3 · The Malibu sequence

`half 11-08 → 18 easy at +7d → 16 w/ 10 @ M at +14d → 13 w/ 3 @ M at +21d → CIM at +28d`

**The recovery premise is SUPPORTED.** After the AFC half (08-16) — a harder
race, 617 ft of climb, avgHr 168 — his own return was clean:

```
+2d   4.01 mi  hr 125        +7d   11.01 mi @ 8:01  hr 147
+4d   4.26 mi  hr 123        +8d    4.02 mi  hr 139
+5d   9.14 mi  hr 156
```

He absorbs a hard half well and is running comfortably by day 7. **This is real
evidence, and it supports racing Malibu and returning promptly.**

**But the specific day-7 dose is not supported.** His demonstrated day-7
post-half run is **11.01 mi**. The proposal asks **18.0** — **+64%** on the only
comparable data point he has.

**Recommendation:** 14–15 mi easy at +7d, not 18. That is still a step up on 11.01
and stays inside what he has shown.

### 4 · 2026-11-22 — 16 mi with 10 at M

**NOT SUPPORTED, and this is the clearest case in the document.**

His entire history of marathon-pace work inside a long run:

| prescribed | what happened |
|---|---|
| 06-27 · 14 mi, **4 @ M** | **completed** — miles 11–14 at 7:14 avg |
| 07-05 · 16 mi, **5 @ M** | **NO RUN. Missed entirely.** |
| 07-25 · (unprescribed) | ~5 mi at ≤MP inside 18, self-directed |

**Two prescribed MP long runs in the whole block. One completed. His
demonstrated maximum MP dose is 4–5 miles.**

10 at M is **double that**, with nothing in between, at **−14 days** from the
goal race. `Research/08` §9.2 asks for 10–12 at MP in the −3 week — so it is
firmly **ALLOWED**. It is not **SUPPORTED**, and the gap is not marginal.

There is also no MP progression between now and then to earn it. The block's
MP ladder currently reads 3+2 → 5+3 → 4 → 5, and the preview would make the −3
week 10 — a doubling with no intermediate rung.

**Recommendation: 16 mi with 6 at M**, which is +20% on his demonstrated 5 and
sits on the progression rather than jumping off it — AND make it evidence-
responsive rather than fixed today (below).

## The sequence, judged as a whole

Two of the four challenged items are individually defensible and become
questionable **only in sequence**:

- 11-01's 21.5 with a fast finish is reasonable **if** the 10-18 twenty-miler
  went clean. It sits 7 days before a race he intends to run hard.
- 11-15's 18 easy is reasonable **if** Malibu was raced at B effort and recovery
  matched 08-16's. It is day 7 post-race.
- 11-22's MP block is reasonable **if** 11-15 was absorbed.

**Each depends on the one before, and every one of those dependencies is
evidence that does not exist yet.** Fixing all three in September is the actual
error — not the numbers themselves.

---

## The late-cycle sessions must stay evidence-responsive

**This is the most important recommendation in the document, and it is an
engineering one, not a coaching one.**

The 11-22 prescription should not be decided today. It should resolve from:

| input | source that already exists |
|---|---|
| Malibu execution | `gradeStimulus` on the 11-08 race row |
| recovery in the following 7 days | `resolveDayExecutions` over 11-09 → 11-15 |
| HR behaviour and decoupling | `assessDeterioration` thirds |
| recent load | `normalWeeklyMileage` |
| missed training | the MISSED state, already distinguished from absent |
| response to the 11-15 long run | the same grader |

Every one of those readers is built and tested. **What is missing is that
nothing re-prices a −3 taper session from them.** `recomputePacesForPlan`
reprices PACE but never re-sizes a dose, and no mechanism exists to say "the MP
dose on 11-22 is 6 miles, or 10, depending on what the previous fortnight
showed."

That is a real gap and I am naming it as a deliverable rather than papering over
it with a fixed number: **DOSE-RESPONSIVE-TAPER**. Until it exists, the honest
choice is to author the dose he has EARNED (6 at M) and revisit it in November
with the evidence in hand — not to author the dose the table allows and hope.

## What I would tell him in one sentence

His data supports the *shape* of this block — fast-finish long runs, racing a
half four weeks out, returning promptly afterwards — and does not yet support
its *peaks*: a 60-mile week, a 21.5-mile run, or a 10-mile MP block, none of
which he has come close to. Earn them in October and the November sessions can
be authored from evidence instead of from a table.
