## What this says, and the two decisions it surfaces

**1 · The path exists and is reachable, and on this history it has not fired.** That is
the honest answer and it is not the same as "it cannot fire". The closest miss above is
a real week, 0.4 mi short of the bar, on a bar that is doctrine's own number and the
same one the downward path uses. Two more weeks cleared it by more than 11 mi and were
refused by Rule 8 for a reason Rule 8 exists to give.

**2 · DECISION FOR THE OWNER · a CUTBACK week he overran by 11 per cent.** 2026-06-01
is the sharpest row in the table: 44.9 mi run against a 40.5 mi prescription, refused
because the plan marked that week a cutback. Rule 8 is unambiguous that a week the
engine authored small is not evidence about the runner's normal, and
`lib/adaptation/canonical/levers/weekly-volume.ts` drops cutback weeks for exactly the
same reason, so this directory is consistent with the existing owner of the question.
But a cutback is not a taper: the runner was not told to rest, he was told to run less,
and he ran more. Whether an OVERRUN cutback should be admissible is a product call, it
changes `weekly-volume.ts` as well as this directory, and an agent should not make it.

**3 · DECISION FOR THE OWNER · the seam.** Everything above is an advisory. Nothing
here can raise a week on his phone while `AUTOMATIC_ADAPTATION_AUTHORITY` is `false`,
and opening it is his call, not an implementation detail.

**4 · Rule 14, measured rather than argued.** 76 merged run-days carrying 946.9 mi sit inside this window. Reading them as 
volume would have manufactured a surplus in most weeks of the year. The canonical
predicate is doing real work on this account, not hypothetical work.
