## Q7 · What IS the active race target today?

The ruling was: *"Do not treat 3:13:30 as the established race-day prescription
today… The active execution target should remain closer to current demonstrated
capacity until the runner bridges the gap through actual training."*

Understood, but the engine needs a number today. Current projection is **3:23:50**,
with a likely range of **3:13:28 – 3:26:51**.

**Which of these should the app show as the active execution target?**

1. The projection itself, ~**3:24** (7:47/mi).
2. The **fast edge of the current-evidence range**, ~3:13:28 — but that is
   effectively 3:13:30, which the ruling just deprecated.
3. Something between, e.g. ~**3:18–3:20**, on the basis that a modest,
   *evidence-supported* improvement is defensible where a ten-minute one is not.
4. A **range rather than a single number** — race to 3:20–3:24 with 3:13–3:15
   named as the conditional upside.

Option 4 is the most honest and the hardest to display well. My default without
an answer: **option 1** (show 3:24 as active, 3:13–3:15 as conditional upside
with its named criteria), because it cannot overstate current capacity.

## Q8 · Does the BASELINE progress marathon-effort pace, or only duration?

Two rulings point in different directions and I want to be sure which is meant.

> *"Do not immediately change all training marathon work from 7:52 to 7:23.
> Progress training from current demonstrated marathon effort. As evidence
> improves, the canonical adaptation engine may move the marathon-training
> anchor."*

> *"The baseline plan must be intrinsically progressive… It must intentionally
> progress: …marathon-effort duration. Marathon-specific pace… A plan that
> merely repeats today's capability fails even if every number is internally
> consistent."*

So which does the **baseline** do?

- **(a) Duration only.** Marathon-effort miles grow 4-5 → 6-8 → 8-10 → 6-8 →
  4-5, all prescribed at ~7:52, and only adaptation ever moves the pace.
- **(b) Duration and a scheduled pace progression.** The baseline also steps the
  marathon-effort pace across the block — say 7:52 → 7:45 → 7:38 — as a stated
  forecast, with adaptation confirming, holding or overriding it.

(b) satisfies "intrinsically progressive" more literally. (a) is safer and puts
every pace change behind observed evidence. **My default: (b), but conservative
— a small scheduled progression, explicitly labelled a forecast with the
assumption named, so adaptation replaces an assumption rather than filling a
vacuum.**

## Q9 · Peak volume shape, and what makes a peak week "earned"

The ruling: *"Prefer repeatable weeks in the low-to-mid 50s over touching 60 and
then collapsing. A 60-mile peak may remain, but it should be one or two earned
peak weeks — not the definition of success."*

Two things follow that I'd rather not guess:

**(i)** Keep the 60 and build more low-50s weeks around it, or **lower the peak
to ~55–56** and raise the floor so more weeks sit in the low 50s? He has **never
run a 50-mile week**, so "repeatable low-to-mid 50s" is itself new territory.

**(ii)** What makes a peak week **earned**, concretely? The plan is authored in
advance, so "earned" has to be either a precondition the preceding weeks must
satisfy, or a decision deferred to adaptation. If it's a precondition, what is
it — e.g. the two preceding weeks completed at ≥90% of prescribed volume with
their key sessions intact?

My default: peak stays at **60 for a single week**, with the surrounding block
sitting in the low-to-mid 50s, and "earned" implemented as a **stated
precondition** in the plan's own explanation rather than a silent one.
