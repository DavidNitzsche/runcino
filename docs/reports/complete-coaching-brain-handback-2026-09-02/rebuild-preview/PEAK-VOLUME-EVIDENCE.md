## 4 · Marathon-specific workload — a second reduction

Dedicated marathon-pace sessions are **identical** in both plans: one 11 mi @ MP
and one 7 mi @ MP tempo. The difference is entirely in what is embedded in long
runs.

| Marathon-pace miles inside long runs | |
|---|---|
| live | 4.0 + 5.5 + 11.0 = **20.5** |
| composed | 5.0 |

The live plan carries a `LONG · 11mi @ M` — eleven continuous marathon-pace
miles inside a long run, the single most race-specific session in the block. The
rebuild drops it. Threshold volume falls the same way, roughly 28 mi to 15 mi
across the block.

For a marathon build, marathon-pace volume inside a long run is the most
specific stimulus there is. **Losing 15.5 miles of it is a material reduction in
specificity**, and combined with §3 it means the rebuild is less demanding than
the live plan on peak weekly, peak long AND marathon specificity — while
starting from a base 9.3 mi/wk higher and a fitness read 3.7 VDOT better.

That is the "fitter runner gets the worse plan" signature, and here it is not
explained away by the thesis.

## 7 · What I recommend

**Retain 58.5 as the peak week.** Justified by his evidence: +20.6% over an
all-time best of 48.5, with no week above 50 ever recorded. Do not raise it to
meet a band derived from a self-declared label.

**Correct the peak long run and the marathon-pace loss before rebuilding.** Both
are reductions his evidence does not support, in a block whose own thesis names
the long run as the priority. Specifically: the peak long should not sit below
his demonstrated 21.5, and the loss of the eleven-mile marathon-pace long run
needs either restoring or an authored reason.

This is not "push harder to satisfy a category." It is the opposite — his
evidence says he can hold 21.5 and has done, and it says nothing at all that
justifies 20.5 in a durability-limited block.
