# Questions for external review · round 4

Ten. Same runner and plan. Weighted toward what blocks the **Adaptation Engine**
build, because the ruling that each lever needs its own evidence contract means
several contracts do not exist yet.

Q20 defined what moves the **threshold** anchor. Q21-Q22 are its missing
siblings. Q23-Q25 are engine mechanics. Q26-Q29 are evidence-admissibility
questions that affect both the plan and adaptation.

**Each carries the default I will build.** "Defaults are fine" is a complete
reply.

---

## Q21 · What evidence may move the WEEKLY VOLUME lever?

Q9 gave the eight conditions for an **earned peak week**. That is one specific
decision. The general question is unanswered: outside the peak-week test, what
lets the engine propose *more weekly volume* than the baseline scheduled?

**Default:** at least three consecutive non-cutback weeks completed at ≥95% of
prescribed volume with their key sessions at FULL or SUBSTANTIAL stimulus, and
the demonstrated-load envelope advanced; movement bounded to **+~5% of the
prescribed week**, never more than one step per cutback cycle.

## Q22 · What evidence may move the LONG-RUN lever?

Same gap. Long-run distance is a separate lever with its own evidence contract,
and the doctrine is explicit that faster threshold work must not authorise a
longer long run.

**Default:** the two most recent prescribed long runs completed at ≥95% of
distance with no meaningful late deterioration, and the `Research/00a` 110%/30-day
spike rule still satisfied at the proposed distance; movement bounded to **+1
mile** per step.

## Q23 · When does adaptation evaluate, and how far ahead may a proposal reach?

Two mechanics questions in one.

**(a) Cadence.** After every completed run? Only after *relevant* sessions (long
runs, quality, races)? At weekly boundaries? A mix?

**(b) Reach.** May a single proposal change only the next week, the next two, or
the remainder of the block? A threshold-pace change logically reprices every
future threshold session; a volume change does not have to.

**Default:** (a) evaluate after relevant completed sessions AND at the weekly
boundary, never mid-session; (b) a pace change reprices all future sessions of
that type, a volume or long-run change reaches only to the next cutback boundary.

## Q24 · What happens when levers disagree?

Threshold evidence says advance; volume evidence says hold. Or marathon-effort
duration is earned while the long run is not.

Does the engine act on each lever independently, or does one HOLD suppress the
others for coherence?

**Default:** levers are independent by design — that is the point of separate
evidence contracts — **except** that a volume or long-run HOLD suppresses any
proposal that would increase total weekly load in the same week. Pace changes
that do not raise load proceed.

## Q25 · Who activates the conditional 3:13-3:15 target, and when?

Q7 named the criteria. It did not say what re-evaluates them, how often, or
whether activation is automatic once satisfied.

Given automatic adaptation is disabled and this is the most consequential number
on the race page, is activation an **owner-approved proposal**, or may the engine
promote it once the criteria are objectively met?

**Default:** owner-approved proposal, re-evaluated at each weekly boundary and
after each tune-up race, with the unmet criteria shown so the runner can see what
is still missing.

## Q26 · Should the CIM execution target be course-adjusted?

CIM is net downhill — roughly 723 ft gain against 1027 ft loss. The engine holds
one canonical downhill-giveback coefficient of **0.50**.

Should the prescribed execution target be **course-adjusted** (faster than the
flat-equivalent projection), or should the target stay flat-equivalent with the
course handled inside the per-mile pacing plan?

**Default:** target stays flat-equivalent; the course is expressed in the
mile-by-mile pacing plan only. Adjusting both would double-count.

## Q27 · What makes a session "representative" enough to be evidence?

Used throughout the Q20 contract but undefined. Which conditions should
disqualify a session, or require adjustment before it counts?

Candidates: heat above some dewpoint · significant elevation · trail or soft
surface · treadmill · wind · altitude · a session run at a deliberately different
effort than prescribed.

**Default:** disqualify treadmill and trail outright for pace evidence; adjust
for heat and grade where the adjustment is trustworthy; disqualify anything with
missing or unreliable segmentation.

## Q28 · Do treadmill runs count as evidence at all?

He runs some — a 3.14 mi treadmill run on 2026-08-27, for instance. Treadmill
pace is not comparable to road pace, but the session still happened and still
carried load.

Should treadmill runs count toward **volume and load** evidence while being
excluded from **pace** evidence? Or excluded entirely?

**Default:** count fully toward volume, load and consistency; **never** toward
pace or threshold evidence.

## Q29 · How should a truncated or incomplete activity be treated?

His 2026-09-02 run was truncated by 0.43 mi by a watch defect. Data-quality
handling matters because earned-peak criterion 5 requires **no unresolved
truncated or misclassified activity data**.

Does a truncated run count toward volume at its recorded distance, get repaired,
or get excluded? And may it ever serve as *pace* evidence for the portion that
was captured?

**Default:** count at recorded distance toward volume with a provenance flag;
usable as pace evidence only for complete, correctly segmented work intervals;
never usable for late-session deterioration, since the late portion is what is
missing.

## Q30 · How should marathon effort be PRESCRIBED to the runner?

Practical, and it affects every marathon-specific session. Three options:

1. **A single pace** — "7:52/mi".
2. **A pace range** — "7:48-7:56/mi".
3. **Effort plus an HR ceiling**, with pace shown as a guide — "marathon effort,
   HR under 160, around 7:52".

Option 3 fits the ruling that marathon-effort work is about sustaining effort
after accumulated fatigue, where a fixed pace late in a 20-miler may be the wrong
instruction. But it is harder to grade objectively.

**Default:** option 2 as the prescription with option 3's HR ceiling attached —
a range to run to, a ceiling not to exceed, and grading against the range with HR
as the corroborating channel.
