## 2. Why 4 × 1 mile

Two bounds, and the tighter one binds.

- **Doctrine band.** `reps: r(3, 6)`, `rep: b(1, 1, 'mi')` — §5.3's own table
  row, checked against the live document text at CI time by
  `VOCAB.threshold-family` (`web-v2/lib/doctrine/registry.ts:12564-12626`).
- **The week's Daniels share.** `sessionAllowanceMi()`
  (`select.ts:193-197`) returns `weeklyMi × AT_PACE_WEEKLY_SHARE_CAP.T`.
  §5.3 caps T at 10% of weekly mileage, and the week is 45.0 mi, so
  **allowance = 4.5 mi at T**.
- **The trajectory's earned dose.** `SLOT-ROTATE-2` passes the
  `OverloadTrajectory`'s `targetAtPaceMinutes` in as a *sizing* ceiling only
  (`generate.ts:5845-5872`).

`fits()` (`select.ts:770-790`) then walks the band down from the top:

```ts
let reps = structure.reps.max;                       // 6
while (reps > structure.reps.min && reps * one > sizeToMi) reps--;
```

With `one = 1 mi` and `sizeToMi = min(4.5, trajectory target)`: 6 > 4.5 → 5;
5 > 4.5 → 4; 4 > 4.5 is false → stop. **reps = 4.**

So the honest answer is: *four, because five miles at threshold would breach
Daniels' 10% weekly share on a 45-mile week, and three is below what the block
has earned.* The rep **length** (1 mi) is the doctrine band's only value —
§5.3's first structure states 1 mi flat, not a range.

## 6. Why the warm-up is 2.1 miles

**It is not a warm-up prescription. It is the day's leftover mileage, halved.**

`spec-builder.ts:1440-1456`, threshold branch:

```ts
const budget   = distance_mi ?? 7;                                  // 8.5
const wuFloor  = Math.max(0.5, Math.min(1.5, budget * 0.3));        // 1.5
const cdFloor  = Math.max(0.5, Math.min(1.0, budget * 0.25));       // 1.0
const reps     = Math.min(repCount, Math.max(2, Math.floor((budget - wuFloor - cdFloor) / repMi)));  // 4
const floatTotalT = Math.max(0, reps - 1) * (restS / 540);          // 3 × 60/540 = 0.3333
const wu = Math.max(wuFloor, (budget - reps * repMi - floatTotalT) / 2);
const cd = Math.max(cdFloor, budget - reps * repMi - floatTotalT - wu);
```

`(8.5 − 4 − 0.3333) / 2 = 2.0833 → 2.1`. Stored value: **2.1**. Exact.

The 8.5-mile day budget is the week's quality-day allocation from the composer.
The warm-up floors (30% of the day, capped at 1.5 mi) are the only doctrine-ish
content; above the floor the number is pure remainder arithmetic. Nothing
consulted a doctrine warm-up duration, the runner's own warm-up habit, or the
session's intensity.

Incidental finding at the same site: `restS / 540` converts jog-recovery seconds
into miles at a **hardcoded 9:00/mi**. That is the same fabricated constant
`P1-47` removed from `expand-spec.ts` in 2026-07-06; it survives here as
mileage accounting. Small — it moves the WU/CD split by hundredths of a mile —
but it is the same class.

## 13. On the precision of "7:19"

The owner's read is correct, and the trace strengthens it rather than merely
supporting it.

`439` is not a measurement. It is `round(443 + (419 − 443) × 0.15)` — a Daniels
formula output off a scalar VDOT, nudged by a hand-set grace constant. It has
**no represented uncertainty of any kind.** The single-second resolution is an
artefact of `Math.round` at the end of a chain whose *first* term is already
13 s/mi away from what his own threshold sessions demonstrate. Presenting it as
a bare point value asserts a precision the input cannot support, and
`Research/04`'s own §5.3 states cruise intervals as a band in every other
dimension (3-6 reps, 1 min per mile) — the pace is the one place the engine
collapses a range it was handed.

Brief 03 is directly on point twice over:

> **Precision should match the workout.** Threshold work may warrant tighter
> targets. … Do not manufacture precision because the software can display it.

"Tighter" licenses a *narrower band*, not the absence of one. And the second
sentence is exactly what a bare 7:19 does.

**The material change since that brief was written** is that the new threshold
resolver returns a **real, computed confidence** — `0.727` here, with
`sourceMode: "direct"` and three named corroborating activities — rather than an
assumption. That is the missing ingredient a band needs in order to be honest
rather than decorative. A band widened by low confidence and narrowed by high
confidence would mean something; a fixed ±5 s/mi would just be a second
arbitrary constant.

Concretely, and consistent with how this app already treats easy pace after the
2026-08-31 decision:

- Render threshold work as a **band whose half-width is a function of the
  resolved confidence**, e.g. `4 × 1 mi · 7:08-7:14/mi` at confidence 0.73,
  widening as confidence falls and collapsing toward a point only at high
  confidence with direct, fresh, corroborated evidence.
- The machinery is already in place on the wire and being thrown away: the
  expanded phase for the work segment carries `tolerancePaceSPerMi: 8`
  (`/api/v5/today` sets `cardTolerance = 8` for threshold,
  `route.ts:1449-1452`), and the watch grades against `439 ± 8` — i.e.
  **7:11-7:27**. The phone has that band in hand and prints only the midpoint.
  So the two surfaces are already describing the session differently, which is a
  Rule 16 problem independent of everything else: *the runner is graded on a
  band he is never shown.*
- The right band is not `439 ± 8` — it is centred on **430**, the number his
  evidence actually supports. Both fixes are needed; showing a band around the
  wrong centre is not an improvement.

My honest assessment: the owner is right that 7:19 overstates its own precision,
and the more serious finding underneath it is that the number is 9 s/mi off in
the first place, for two separate and independently fixable reasons.

The structurally honest fix is for a warm-up to be a real segment with a ramp
(start pace, end pace, or simply "easy, last quarter mile building"), rather than
a flat target with prose bolted on. That is a change to the wire contract and the
expander, and is not something to do in a read-only trace.

## 15. The cool-down string — FIXED

Found at `web-v2/lib/training/spec-card.ts:139`. The real string was:

> `cooldown: 'Easy jog. Do not skip it, it shortens tomorrow.'`

Close to the owner's recollection and worth fixing on its own terms: "it shortens
