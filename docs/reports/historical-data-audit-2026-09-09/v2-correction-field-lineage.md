### 1.6 Phase boundaries (start/end timestamps per phase, distinct from duration) — confirmed absent

- **The question, precisely.** v1 established `phases` population (52/162) but never inspected the phase object's own key set. ### 1.7 Pause/resume events (the event itself, not just the elapsed/moving delta)

Two **entirely independent** pipelines exist, neither of which persists the discrete event.

- **Pipeline A — treadmill (`BeltTracker`).** `native-v2/Faff/Faff/BeltTracker.swift:151-190` — `pausedSec`/`droppedSec`/`unmeasuredSec` are pure running `Double` accumulators. `resync(to:)` is called "on pause, on resume, and on every tick while the session is not running" and simply credits elapsed wall time into the aggregate — there is **no array of individual pause/resume events with timestamps kept anywhere in the struct**. **[SOURCE]**, current tip, direct read of the struct's full field list and the `resync` doc comment. This is the mechanism feeding `clockAudit.pausedSec`/`declinedSec` that v1 already covered at the aggregate level (its own field-lineage correction, §1 of the final handback) — this pass adds the confirmation that the *event itself* never existed to begin with, on either side of v1's false-invariant finding: even if the two TS readers stopped discarding the field tomorrow, there is still no way to answer "how many times did RUNNER_DAVID pause, and when" — only "how many total seconds."
- **Pipeline B — outdoor/GPS (`HealthKitImporter`).** `native-v2/Faff/Faff/HealthKitImporter.swift:1113-1147` — `HKWorkoutEvent` on an Apple Watch workout carries **discrete, wall-clock-timestamped** pause/resume pairs, and the app explicitly distinguishes two channels: `.pause`/`.resume` (user-initiated — long-press or tap X) and `.motionPaused`/`.motionResumed` (the watch's automatic stop-detection — "red lights, water stops, traffic — the most common pause source for a runner who never manually touches the watch"). `pauseRanges(in:)` builds `[(Date, Date)]` from these events with real start/end timestamps. **[SOURCE]**, current tip, direct read of the function and its own doc comment (which cites a real dated defect: "David's 6mi easy summed-splits showed 52:40 vs actual 50:34, 126s of auto-pause time leaking into mile 6").
- **What happens to Pipeline B's data: computed into a correction, then discarded.** `pauseRanges` is consumed by `unpaused(from:to:pauses:)` purely to correct per-mile split *durations* (subtracting overlap). **The ranges themselves — the actual pause/resume wall-clock timestamps, and the manual-vs-automatic distinction — are never sent to the server and never persisted.** Confirmed by absence: none of the 79 distinct top-level `data` keys, and none of the phase-object keys enumerated in §1.6, name anything pause-shaped for outdoor runs. **[PROD-QUERY]** (key-set exhaustive) + **[SOURCE]** (no payload construction site anywhere sends `pauseRanges`' output).
- **Net finding.** For outdoor/GPS runs, richer pause data exists on-device (with a manual/automatic distinction the treadmill pipeline doesn't even have) than for treadmill runs, and it is thrown away *earlier* in the pipeline — at the point of derivation, before ever reaching a network payload — whereas the treadmill's `pausedSec` at least reaches the server and gets discarded there (per v1's already-documented false-invariant finding). These are two structurally different data-loss shapes for the same underlying question ("did the runner pause, and for how long/why"), and no domain in v1 traced the outdoor half at all.
- **CONSUMERS.** Pipeline A: `web-v2/app/api/watch/workouts/complete/route.ts` (per v1). Pipeline B: none past `unpaused()` itself — the corrected split durations are the only consumer, and even those don't carry a "this split was pause-corrected" flag through to storage.
- **UI SURFACES.** `[BLOCKED: not checked]` for both pipelines — no render performed.
