What was wrong. The largest token in every cell was the date — the one thing you already know. A sixteen-mile long run and a rest day rendered as pixel-identical boxes, so load was not encoded anywhere. Hard versus easy was a single eight-pixel dot with no legend on the page. And the only genuinely week-level numbers sat outside the cells as eleven-pixel muted text.

Why a row of dates is the wrong shape. Seven equal cells assert that all seven days matter equally. A training week is one or two sessions that matter and four that are filler plus rest. The grid took the highest-variance thing about a week — how load is distributed across it — and drew it as a constant.

  • Width is load. The shape of the bar is the shape of the week. Sunday being the long one is visible without reading a digit.
  • Rest days are thin gaps, not full cells demanding attention. That is what a rest day is: absence of load.
  • Fill versus outline answers done-versus-coming; the now marker answers how far through you are.
  • Amber marks the session that matters — quality, or the long run — so "where is the hard day" is answered by weight, not an unlabelled dot.
  • It gives back roughly sixty pixels of vertical space.

One deliberate restraint

Train already has a better week view than this: a vertical list with day, type, distance and pace. That is the right depth for a planning page. Today gets the glanceable load bar, Train keeps the list, and we do not build a third representation of the same week.

03What changed on the rest of the page

Five changes, each traceable to a measurement rather than a preference.

  • The prescription gets the widest column. Today it has the narrowest — 300px against 480px for the card that merely restates it. That single inversion in one CSS line is why your hero numbers clip to "TARGET PA…" while the duplicate renders large and clean beside them.
  • The restatement is deleted. Distance, duration and effort appeared three times across three adjacent cards; the word "easy" appeared five times on one screen. The hero owns the numbers, the session card owns the breakdown, the plan card owns the targets. Each datum has exactly one home.
  • Estimated time leaves the hero. It already lives in the session card. Two stats instead of three means nothing truncates at any width.
  • The race becomes one row. It earns the hero on race day and the morning after; from then on it is a line with its result, its provenance, and a way back into the story.
  • Nothing pops in. The morning line moved into the header and every remaining conditional element has its space reserved, so the page does not rearrange itself as data arrives. Layout shift was doing as much damage as duplication.
  • Tiles must earn their place. Weekly volume rendering "0 mi" against a finished block's bars, and a training-form ring you would never act on, are gone. A tile that cannot say something true today does not render.

No cards that pop in

The morning line keeps its words and loses its box. It was a card that appeared only on some days and shoved everything under it down the page. Now it sits in the header as part of the page's fixed structure, with its space reserved whether or not there is anything to say. Same rule everywhere: nothing above the fold may appear conditionally and reflow the screen. If a thing is sometimes there, its space is always there.

04What I need from you

The week bar is the only piece here that is a genuine design decision rather than a measured correction. Everything else follows from the audit and I would just build it.

Say go and it ships behind the same gates as everything else today. Say no and the strip stays as it is while the rest of the page still improves.
