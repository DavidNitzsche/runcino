## Plain-English meaning

The app must begin with the runner's demonstrated fitness, but it must not end there. Current
fitness tells the coach what the runner can safely absorb today. The product's job is to use
training to increase that capacity, recognize when the increase has been earned, and ask for more.

A plan that repeatedly prescribes only what the runner has already proved can maintain fitness. It
is not delivering the central promise of this app. The runner should become able to run farther,
faster, more consistently, or with greater control because the plan progressively created those
adaptations.

The governing relationship is:

> Train the runner you have. Build the runner they want to become.

"Train the runner you have" defines the safe and honest starting point. "Build the runner they want
to become" defines the direction and purpose. Neither half can replace the other.

## What "push" means

Pushing means applying enough new training stress to create adaptation, then progressing it when
the runner demonstrates that they can absorb it. Depending on the evidence and goal, that may mean:

- faster prescribed pace;
- more weekly volume;
- longer sustained work at the relevant intensity;
- more repetitions or longer work intervals;
- shorter recovery or greater session density;
- a longer long run or more race-specific work;
- advancing from general conditioning toward the specific demands of the runner's goal; or
- restoring progression promptly after recovery, interruption, or a temporary hold.

The app should not move all of these at once. Pace, duration, volume, and density are separate
questions with different evidence. Progress one primary stressor at a time unless the owning
doctrine explicitly supports a combined change.

## What "push" does not mean

It does not mean prescribing the runner's aspirational goal pace as if they already possess that
capacity. The goal shapes the trajectory; demonstrated evidence sizes the next step.

It does not mean weakening injury, recovery, readiness, or load-tolerance guards to manufacture
progress. The app should spend safe headroom, not pretend the guardrails do not exist.

It does not mean making the plan harder merely because a new calendar week began. The calendar can
propose progression, but the runner earns it through execution and recovery.

It does not mean treating every completed workout as proof that more is appropriate. The app must
consider control, consistency, physiological cost, late-session deterioration, soreness, readiness,
and the surrounding load.

It does not mean silently changing tomorrow's workout, restructuring the plan, or changing a race
goal. The runner should understand the evidence, the proposed progression, and what it changes.

## Current fitness is a floor, not a ceiling

Current fitness is the safety floor from which the next prescription is built. It prevents the
goal, optimism, or one exceptional workout from inflating what the runner has actually demonstrated.
It must not become a permanent ceiling that forces every future week to repeat the same load and
pace.

Once evidence shows that the runner has gained capacity or tolerance, continuing to prescribe the
old level is a coaching failure. The app should update its belief, explain the evidence, and spend
some of the newly demonstrated headroom.

The amount spent should be large enough to matter and small enough to remain inside the safety
owner's boundaries. A progression so small or infrequent that it never changes the training
stimulus is not meaningful progression.

## The upward coaching loop

The app needs a reliable positive loop, not only mechanisms for holding, cutting, or recovering:

1. **Prescribe a purposeful stimulus.** The workout or week targets a specific adaptation tied to
   the runner's goal.
2. **Observe actual execution.** Capture what the runner did, how controlled it was, its
   physiological cost, and the relevant subjective context.
3. **Interpret the evidence.** Decide whether it demonstrates capacity, load tolerance, durability,
   incomplete absorption, or an anomalous day.
4. **Update the runner model.** Increase confidence in the relevant capability without allowing the
   goal itself to inflate the estimate.
5. **Choose the progression axis.** Decide independently whether pace, duration, volume, density, or
   a hold is supported.
6. **Propose a meaningful next step.** Explain what was earned and what will change.
7. **Apply with awareness.** Consequential changes reach the runner through an explicit choice or
   clear notice, according to the product rules.
8. **Verify the delivered plan.** Confirm the new prescription reached the actual iPhone and Watch
   experience rather than existing only in backend state.
9. **Repeat.** The new training becomes evidence for the next decision.

If the loop stops after observation, the app is a tracker. If it only updates an internal fitness
number, it is an analytics product. If it adapts only downward, it is a safety system. The product
becomes a coach when the evidence reliably changes future training in both directions.

## Evidence that should earn progression

No single metric should act as a universal "make it harder" switch. Evidence should match the axis
being progressed.

**Pace progression may be supported by:**

- repeated controlled execution at the current prescription;
- direct race, threshold, or high-intensity evidence appropriate to the pace domain;
- stable or improving physiological cost at the same external workload;
- consistent pacing without late-session collapse; and
- enough confidence that the result is not one exceptional or misclassified effort.

**Duration or volume progression may be supported by:**

- consistent completion of the recent load;
- adequate recovery between sessions;
- stable durability late in long or sustained work;
- no meaningful injury or illness signal;
- demonstrated tolerance of the current weekly and long-run load; and
- adherence sufficient to make the prior block interpretable.

**Density progression may be supported by:**

- controlled work at the current recovery interval;
- evidence that shortening recovery will improve the intended stimulus rather than merely increase
  fatigue; and
- surrounding-week capacity to absorb the denser session.

Subjective feedback matters, but it is noisy. Use it as evidence alongside execution and recovery,
especially when repeated or corroborated, rather than treating one confident or discouraged answer
as decisive.

## When the app should hold or reduce

Holding is an active coaching decision when the desired adaptation is still being developed, the
evidence is ambiguous, or another stressor has just progressed. Under ambiguity, David's ruling is
to offer a meaningful choice.

Reducing is appropriate when the runner is not absorbing the load, recovery is incomplete, injury or
illness risk is credible, or actual execution shows that the prescription is currently too costly. A
reduction should state the reason and preserve the path back upward. Temporary recovery must not
silently become a permanent lower trajectory.

After repeated non-adherence, the app should ask for recommitment or recalibration. It should not
keep rewriting the plan around behavior that makes its evidence uninterpretable.

## What the runner should experience

The runner should periodically receive a clear message such as:

> You have handled the last three weeks with control, and the final miles of your long runs are
> staying steady. You are ready for a little more marathon work. Next week's long run keeps the same
> total distance and extends the marathon-pace section by one mile.

That message contains four things: the evidence; the capability that improved; the specific
progression being proposed; and what remains controlled.

This is how the app creates the intended moment:

> "Oh shit… I've gotten better at running."

The app should also make non-progression understandable:

> Your pace is ready to move, but this week already increases the long run. We are holding the pace
> for one more week so only one main stressor changes.

The runner should never have to infer progression from a changed number or discover it accidentally
when opening tomorrow's workout.

## Programme requirements

1. Every adaptation audit must measure upward opportunities, holds, and reductions separately.
2. The programme must track how often an eligible upward progression is recognized, proposed,
   accepted, delivered, and successfully absorbed.
3. A progression mechanism does not count as built merely because a function or rule exists. It must
   place meaningful progression on real generated calendars.
4. Golden-runner and doctrine tests must include runners who should progress in pace, volume,
   duration, and density, plus runners who should hold or reduce.
5. Tests must fail if safety evidence is bypassed and also fail if valid headroom is repeatedly left
   unused.
6. Calendar templates cannot be the final authority. They propose the next stimulus; the runner's
   evidence decides whether it advances, holds, or changes shape.
7. Goal feasibility and current capacity remain separate. The goal determines direction and
   specificity, while current evidence determines the safe next step.
8. Every accepted progression must be traceable through runner-model evidence, plan mutation,
   explanation, cache/version behavior, and the delivered native surface.
9. Progression after time off, illness, or a race must include a defined route back to forward
   training. Recovery is a bridge, not the product destination.
10. Major changes to the progression model, plan structure, or product concept come to David for a
    decision. Routine execution of these settled principles does not.

## Questions for every proposed coaching change

- What capability is this trying to improve?
- What evidence says the runner can safely take the next step?
- Which axis is changing: pace, volume, duration, or density?
- Is another primary stressor changing at the same time?
- Does the change materially increase the intended stimulus?
- Which canonical owner makes this decision?
- Which safety guard constrains it?
- Does the runner understand what was earned and what will change?
- Can the runner choose when the evidence is genuinely ambiguous?
- Will the changed prescription reach the current phone and Watch plan?
- What future evidence will show whether the progression worked?

## Acceptance test for the product promise

For a runner who follows the plan, executes the relevant work with control, and recovers
adequately, the system must eventually produce a meaningful upward change in the appropriate
training axis, explain why it was earned, and deliver it to the runner's active plan without
breaking safety or surprising them.

**If the same runner can complete months of training while the app repeatedly observes improvement
but never increases an appropriate stimulus, the app has failed this brief even if every safety
check passes.**
